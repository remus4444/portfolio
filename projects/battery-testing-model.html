FSAE Battery Testing and Simulation

Started Formula Buckeyes' first battery testing and simulation program, building cell and pack models from lab data so the team can predict how a pack will perform before it's built.

The problem

Before this program, the team chose cells from datasheets and hand calculations. We never tested candidate cells ourselves, and we had no cell models to show how a given cell would affect the car's performance.

We also had no way to work out cooling requirements. That gap showed in the FB26 pack, which paired high-resistance cells with passive cooling. To keep the cells below a safe temperature, the car needed a large powertrain derate.

What I did

I came up with the idea for the program and did all of the work for its first five months. I wrote the standard test procedures and built the models and the MATLAB scripts that process the data. I then trained other leads and members to use them.

Testing

I ran hybrid pulse power characterization (HPPC) tests on the 1s5p Enepaq VTC6 modules used in the FB26 pack, and on candidate cells for future packs. We used the university's lab equipment, including Arbin battery testers, with thermocouples and thermal cameras to measure how temperature was distributed across the cells.

Alongside HPPC, we ran capacity tests and current profile tests. A current profile test replays the current draw of a vehicle event, such as the endurance race, so we can see its effect on battery voltage and temperature and check how well the model matches.

Modeling

I post-processed the raw test data in MATLAB and used it to build cell models in Simulink. The cell models scale up to the full pack, so the team can simulate how a pack will perform before it's built.

Results

The testing changed how the team develops batteries. Mechanical engineers are now much more involved in cell selection, packaging, layout and cooling.

It started with the FB26 pack. Testing its modules let me build a pack model early in the season, and the model showed that the pack needed active cooling to be competitive. Its existing design had no room for it. Knowing that, the team began working toward a battery redesign for 2028, built on what we learned over two seasons of building our first pack.

For that redesign, the team tested eight candidate cells with HPPC across different loads, states of charge and temperatures. The resulting models showed how much a new pack could improve the car once we selected the best cell. The gain was larger than anything else on the car could offer, which made it clear that cell testing and modeling are the key to the car's performance in the years ahead.

What I'd do differently

I'd break the system down into its parts before building a model of it. My early model of the FB26 pack left out the bus bar connections between modules, because I assumed their effect was small. Later testing showed they were what had been throwing the model off. If I had checked whether they were significant from the start, I'd have had an accurate model much sooner.
